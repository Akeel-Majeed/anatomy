# Anatomy

This skill shows a biological structure, or a system of structures such as the limbic system, as a model the reader can turn: the real mesh of every named part, from an open anatomical atlas, lit in its tissue tone, in a framed card with a title, a hint and a live readout, each part carrying its number as a tag, named on hover, with a key under the card and the atlas cited in the caption. The reader learns the structure because the atlas is the model: nothing an atlas provides as a mesh is drawn by hand. For what no atlas has as a mesh, a cell type, a circuit, a cut section with its layers, the skill builds an isometric teaching model on a stand instead, the section route: a matte cast with one face cut to the section the textbooks use, every named part its own solid or band with a numbered pin. Either way, every name and number the figure shows, in the key and the readout, comes from the same truth table that builds its parts.

Thirteen complete examples ship with the skill. `examples/brain/` is the one to open first for any 3D figure and `examples/hippocampus-section/` for any section figure. The others are machines built with the same kit; each is the reference for a technique, so open the one that has the technique you need. Each framework-free example is built with `node examples/<name>/build.mjs`, which writes a standalone HTML page next to the script; add `--light` for the light theme.

- `examples/brain/` is the reference for the 3D route: the whole human brain by lobe from BodyParts3D, nine numbered parts (frontal, parietal, temporal and occipital lobes, insula, cerebellum, midbrain, pons, medulla oblongata), the left and right copies of a lobe one entry. `truth.mjs` names each part's atlas ids, `check.mjs` holds `checkTruth` and its self-test, `build.mjs` packs the committed meshes and `kit/mesh-view.js` into one page, and `scripts/view-check.mjs` proves every part visible and pickable from six views. Drag turns it, hover names a part, arrows step the key, Q hides the names.
- `examples/hippocampus-3d/` is the reference for a region with its neighbours, and for a figure that opens from a chosen view: the human hippocampus with the amygdala, fornix and parahippocampal gyrus from BodyParts3D, four numbered parts on one card, opening from above-left (`FRAME.open`) because the hippocampus is hidden behind the amygdala from the front.
- `examples/limbic-system/` is the reference for a system on one card: the human limbic system from BodyParts3D, eight numbered parts (hippocampus, fornix, mammillary body, thalamus, hypothalamus, amygdala, cingulate gyrus, parahippocampal gyrus), each its own mesh in its tissue tone, so the reader sees how the parts sit together.
- `examples/basal-ganglia/` is the reference for a system the atlas has only in part: the human basal ganglia from BodyParts3D, four numbered parts (caudate nucleus, putamen, globus pallidus, thalamus), with the caption saying that the substantia nigra, the subthalamic nucleus and the nucleus accumbens are not in the atlas.
- `examples/hippocampus-section/` is the reference for the section route: the hippocampal formation in coronal section as a teaching model lying in a cradle on a stand, its regions prisms along the long axis, its layers bands on the cut face, one numbered pin per part, the key generated from `truth.mjs`, the trisynaptic circuit's cords pulsing in order, and `node build.mjs --audit` proving the geometry at rest and with each region pulled out.
- `examples/arcade-cabinet/` is the reference for a concave prism along any axis (its side wall, `prismPaths`), a pulse running chunk by chunk along a cable, a camera placed so a cutaway's inside stays in view, and live parts drawn in reserved slots of the settled order. About 240 solids and 15 cables; `--audit` proves it.
- `examples/raptor-engine/` is the reference for parts that lift apart on hover with staggered springs and per-frame depth re-sorting, for the audit (`kit/audit.mjs`) at rest, apart and in every frame between, for dense pipework from `kit/tube.mjs`, and for smooth shading and fades. A WebGL plume renders between its back and front SVG.
- `examples/desk-computer/` is the reference for drawing in the plane of a face (its CRT, with `sideMatrix`): a cut face's layers and numbers use the same matrices. About 85 solids.
- `examples/ripple-tank/` is the reference for a high camera (44°) that looks into something open, and for WebGL layers registered to the isometric planes and driven by a pure model.
- `examples/dial-indicator/` is the smallest, about 300 lines: the reference for the vanilla loop, the idle tour, pointer mapping and the keyboard.
- `examples/test-rig/` and `examples/spot-plate/` are production React/Next figures: the server/client split, element props, and one control driving many parts.
- `examples/turning-dial/` is the kit's optional turning mode: kit-drawn parts that turn in true 3D, materials, a hinge chain and a free body.

The brain is the quality bar for a 3D figure: its tones, its tags and its key. The hippocampus section is the bar for a section figure: its cut face, pins and key. The test rig is the bar for the drawing itself, and the Raptor for shader work.

## The one idea that matters

**The atlas is the model.** Don't draw boxes, arrows and labels, don't invent a stand-in object, and don't draw by hand anything an atlas provides as a mesh. Show the real structure as the atlas has it, each named part its own mesh in its own tissue tone with its own numbered tag; or, where no atlas has it, draw it as a well-made teaching model, true to a cited source, with every named part its own solid, or for a layer its own band, and its own pin. Here is how a structure maps to a model:

| Structure | Route | Model |
|---|---|---|
| An organ or a region with named parts (the brain by lobe, the hippocampus with its neighbours, the brainstem) | 3D | One atlas mesh per part in its tissue tone, rotatable, each with a numbered tag |
| A nucleus and what surrounds it (the amygdala, the thalamus, the caudate nucleus) | 3D | The nucleus's mesh with its neighbours' meshes around it; stepping to it dims the rest |
| A system or circuit asked for as its parts (the limbic system, the basal ganglia, the Papez circuit) | 3D | One card with every member part the atlas has, each its own mesh in its tissue tone, and the missing members named in the caption |
| The hippocampal subfields (CA1, CA2, CA3, dentate gyrus, subiculum) | Section | Not in the human atlas as meshes, so a coronal section with each subfield its own prism and its layers as bands, as `examples/hippocampus-section/` does |
| A tract (the fornix, the corpus callosum) | 3D when the atlas has it | A mesh in the tract tone |
| A pathway between parts (the perforant path, the corticospinal tract's course) | Section | A bundle of cords from one named part to another, each end on a synapse dot |
| A neuron or a cell | Section | Soma, dendrites, axon hillock, an axon with myelin collars and nodes, terminals; glia as their own bodies |
| A circuit's pathways (the trisynaptic circuit, the basal ganglia loops) | Section | Pathways on a model, with a pulse that runs them in order |
| A layered structure or a cut section (the cortex, the retina, the hippocampus in coronal section) | Section | A block whose layers are bands on the cut face, each band a named part |

Three ways this fails:

- **Wrong.** A name not in the atlas or the source, or a border or a connection the source does not support. This is the only failure that costs the reader an exam mark, and the truth table and the anatomy check exist to catch it.
- **Invented.** A structure drawn by hand when an atlas has its mesh. Drawn geometry does not read as tissue and is less accurate than the atlas; the first section figure read as a bearing for this reason. Take the mesh.
- **Mechanical.** Machine vocabulary on tissue: bevels, screws and knurls, or a region built from arcs of one circle and boxes on a drum. On the section route the stand is machined; the cast is not.

The fix for the first is never drawing a named part the truth table doesn't have, and never typing a part's name anywhere but the truth table. The fix for the second is `node scripts/atlas.mjs <atlas> --find <name>` before any geometry. The fix for the third, on the section route, is a recognisable, well-made model built from many small, precise, purposeful parts.

**Every section figure is highly detailed.** On the 3D route the atlas supplies the detail. On the section route it is your work, and it holds for every figure, however small the structure or the request: a quick figure of one cell is still a dense, real model on a real stand, never a blob on a slab. What scales with the size (Section route, step 1) is how far you push it: a Figure gets the 4× sweep over the cast and its pins, a Hero or an Epic over the whole drawing.
- **Read the real thing.** Build it the way the structure is built: every layer, every border, every fibre bundle with its ends, the sulcus that separates two parts, and the stand, clamp, label plate and pins that hold and name them.
- **Two looks.** At 1× it reads as one clear model. At 4× every part of the drawing still has something true to find: a layer's band, a cord in its groove, a pin's number. A bare face or an empty corner is unfinished work, not calm.
- **Purposeful, never noise.** Detail comes from parts the source names or the stand needs. Each one sits on another part, ends on an edge, and fades rather than stops.
- **Check it last.** Before you finish, zoom to 4× (over the cast and pins for a Figure, the whole drawing for a Hero or an Epic) and add the parts the source names or the stand needs wherever an area of the drawing is plain.

## Workflow

Work in this order. The route and the truth table come before any geometry. Most of the quality is decided there.

1. **Read the request and decide the route.** 3D when the atlas has every requested part as a mesh: BodyParts3D for the human body (`references/neuro.md`, "Sources", lists the ids for the common requests); the skill is human-only for now, and `atlas.mjs allen` and the Allen mouse atlas are there for later (`references/neuro.md`, "Later: the mouse atlas"). A system or circuit asked for as its parts (the limbic system, the basal ganglia, the Papez circuit) is one 3D card of every member the atlas has, with the members it lacks named in the caption (`references/neuro.md`, "Systems"). Section route for cell types, circuits with pathways between parts, cut sections with layers, and any part the atlas has no mesh for (the human hippocampal subfields, the substantia nigra, the subthalamic nucleus, the nucleus accumbens). A mixed request gets the 3D figure of what the atlas has, and a sentence saying what it lacks; add a section card only when the user asks. Then tell the user the route and the time: a 3D figure of parts the atlas has takes minutes (the fetch on first use, the truth table, the build, the checks), a whole brain by lobe included, so it is never an Epic. A section figure is sized as a Figure, a Hero or an Epic (Section route, step 1).
2. **Truth first.** Run `node scripts/atlas.mjs bp3d --find <name>` for every requested name, and write `truth.mjs` beside the build script, a pure module the build imports: `SOURCE` (`title`, `url`, `licence`, `fetched`), `PARTS` (one row per named part: `n`, `id`, `name`, `synonyms`, `atlas: { db, ids, names }`, `tone`, `source`; the left and right copies of a part are one row with two ids) and `PATHS` (empty for a 3D figure). `name` is the atlas's English name with the side stripped and the first letter capitalised; `atlas.names` records what the atlas calls each id, and the check compares it with the atlas's own list. If the user pasted a label list, the user's names are used in `name`, in the user's order, and the atlas's stay in `atlas.names`. Say now which requested parts the atlas does not have, before any geometry.
3. **Fetch and pack.** `node scripts/atlas.mjs bp3d FMA62493 FMA61841 --out examples/<name>/meshes` (`--max-faces` thins a part over the budget; `allen 382 423` in place of the ids is the same command on the mouse atlas, for later). The first run downloads the archive into `~/.cache/anatomy/<atlas>/` (`$ANATOMY_CACHE` overrides) and reuses them after; each part becomes `<out>/<id>.json`, packed by `kit/mesh.mjs`, and the command prints the atlas's name for each id. Commit the packed meshes with a `LICENCE.md` naming the atlas and its licence, so the build and CI run offline. The build never fetches.
4. **Write `build.mjs` from the brain example.** It reads `meshes/*.json`, runs `checkTruth(PARTS, meshes, atlasNames)` from `check.mjs` (copy the brain's, or import it as the hippocampus-3d example does), builds the card with `k.plateHtml` (`fig`, `title`, `hint` of `DRAG TO TURN · HOVER A PART · ARROWS STEP · Q QUIZ`, a `caption` citing the atlas, its licence and `SOURCE.fetched`) around a stage `<div class="an-stage" tabindex="0" role="img" aria-label="…"><canvas class="an-gl"></canvas><div class="an-tags"></div><span class="an-sr" role="status" aria-live="polite"></span></div>`, generates the key from `PARTS` as `<span class="iso-key" data-key><b class="an-n">n</b> <span class="an-name">name</span></span>` per row in order with a `<button class="an-quiz">Quiz</button>`, embeds the meshes as one `<script type="application/json" id="an-data">` holding `{ frame, parts: [{ n, id, name, tone, meshes }] }` with the atlas's `frame` from `references/neuro.md` (plus `open: [azimuth, elevation]` when the front view hides the subject), inlines `kit/mesh-view.js` (read it with `readFileSync`; `scripts/inline-kit.mjs` does not know it) in a module script that calls `mountAnatomy(stage, data)`, and writes the page with `k.pageHtml`; `--light` writes the `-light` page. A figure is one card, a system included; a page with a second atlas would be two cards, each stage mounted separately, numbers restarting at 1 per card, for later.
5. **Verify.** `node examples/<name>/check.mjs` prints `check.mjs: self-test passed`; the build runs `checkTruth` and fails naming the part on a duplicate `n` or `id`, a part with no mesh or a mesh with no part, a mesh with no triangles or a non-finite coordinate, an `atlas.names` entry the atlas's list does not match, or a page over 10 MB. `node scripts/view-check.mjs examples/<name>/<name>.html` must print `view-check: ok`: every part visible from at least one of six views, every visible tag's anchor picking its own part, a clean console, frames fast enough. Then capture the front, the left and the top with `scripts/capture.mjs` and read them by eye against the atlas's own viewer: anterior toward the viewer, superior up, the tones distinct, no tag over the wrong part; if the model opens facing the wrong way, the frame entry is wrong, not the mesh. Drive the hover readout (`n · Name`), the arrows, Q and a drag with `scripts/drive.mjs`. Build and read both themes.
6. **Deliver one file, and say what the atlas does not have.** The page opens offline with no dependencies. Tell the user which requested parts were not in the atlas, and that the names follow the atlas (or the pasted list) with the licence the caption cites.

### Section route

For a cell type, a circuit or a cut section with layers. Write the truth table, the presentation and the parts list before any geometry.

1. **Size it before you start.** Tell the user which size the request is and roughly how long it will take, before any other work.
   - **Figure**: one structure with 6–12 named parts and no cut face with layers (a neuron with its glia, one nucleus), 40–80 parts, SVG with at most one WebGL layer. About 30–90 minutes. Build it without asking.
   - **Hero**: a region with a cut face, pathways and parts that lift on hover (the hippocampal formation in section, the spinal cord with a reflex arc, the basal ganglia with both pathways), 80–150 parts, or one part that turns. About 2–4 hours. Say so, then build it unless the user asked for something quick.
   - **Epic**: a mid-sagittal section with everything named, a multi-region circuit, or the turning mode on more than one part. 6 hours or more. Do not start it. Give the estimate, propose a Hero version that keeps the core of the structure (one region, one loop), and list what the full version would add. Build the Epic only when the user chooses it. A whole brain by lobe is not an Epic: it is a 3D figure of nine parts.
   - Whatever the size, have a working page that opens in the browser within the first hour, and deepen it from there. Run `date` at the start and at each step, and when a step runs past twice its share of the estimate, tell the user where it stands and what is left.
2. **Truth first.** Before any parts list, fetch the source and write `truth.mjs` beside the build script, a pure module the build imports (the test rig's `rig.ts` is the same idea for numbers). Fetch with WebFetch, which needs no key; the build never fetches, so `truth.mjs` is a committed snapshot with its fetch date. Wikipedia is the default source: the structure's article, then each sub-part's article where the main one lacks its borders or connections. Take the name from the infobox (the English name that heads the article, with the infobox's Latin, Terminologia Anatomica or NeuroNames term kept as a synonym), not from the prose; take borders and connections from the prose; use NeuroNames, the Allen Brain Atlas or Gray's on Wikisource for a border the first source leaves unclear. `truth.mjs` exports `SOURCE` (`title`, `url`, `fetched`, `convention`), `PARTS` (one row per named part: `n`, `id`, `name`, `synonyms`, `borders`, `source`, and `layerOf` for a layer that is a band on another part's face) and `PATHS` (`id`, `name`, `from`, `to`, `source`) in circuit order. Every row has its own source URL. If the user pasted a label list, those names, that count and that order are used verbatim, `SOURCE.convention` says where they came from, and fetching only fills borders and paths. You cannot fetch textbooks: when the user names one, ask for that figure's labels pasted. When the table is written, read the source a second time, row by row, and fix every row it does not support. `references/neuro.md` has the schema, the sources and how to read them.
3. **Choose the presentation.** Pick the cut the textbooks use (coronal for the hippocampus, transverse for the spinal cord, sagittal for the cerebellum), decide which parts lift on hover (regions do; a layer lifts with its region), and say in one sentence what moves and what that motion means: for a circuit, the pulse along its pathways in order. Never copy a reference image: take its view and which parts it labels, not its drawing; the names come from the truth table.
4. **Parts list.** Write at least 40 parts (40–80 for a Figure, 80–150 for a Hero, more for an Epic), grouped: stand (base plate, feet, groove, corner screws, label plate, post, clamp), cast (one solid per `PARTS` row that is not a `layerOf` row, the cut face, each `layerOf` row as a band on its parent's face), pins (one per `PARTS` row: head, stem where the face is horizontal, and number), pathways (one cord per `PATHS` row, in a groove, ending on synapse dots) and controls (the quiz toggle). Each part sits on another part, and each named part is one row of the truth table. `references/neuro.md` has the vocabulary with recipes; `references/craft.md` has the stand's.
5. **Plan the world.** Use world units of about 1 px of the real thing: x and y on the floor, z up. Lay out plans as rounded rectangles `{x, y, w, d, r}` and circles. Choose the camera: azimuth 45° by default, 35–60° to show the long side, elevation 30° (higher, like the ripple tank's 44°, to look into something open). Then fit with `fitProjection(points, width, height, { pad, azimuth })`.
6. **Build geometry once.** Precompute every static path in a geometry module with the kit: `slabOf`, `cylinder`, `extrude`, `planOutline`, `topTicks`, `knurl`, `coil`, `radialTicks` and so on. Round parts on any axis (bells, flanges, valves, actuators) come from `kit/lathe.mjs`, and pipes and cables from `kit/tube.mjs`. Moving parts are built once at a rest position and moved with `translateAlong`. Only parts whose shape changes are rebuilt per frame. Each region is its own solid (a prism along the cast's long axis, following the hippocampus section's `prismPaths`, or a lathe), so it can lift on hover and the audit can order it; a layer is a flat fill in its parent's face plane (`references/neuro.md`, "The cut face"), and a pin's head carries its number as `faceTextSvg` on `sideMatrix` or `topMatrix`.
7. **Paint back to front, bottom to top.** Sort repeated parts with `depthOf`. Split anything that wraps another part into a back half and a front half: the springs around a rod, the bars of a frame around a carriage. A moving part is drawn in the slot where it physically sits. See "Painter's order" in `references/craft.md`. Once a figure has pipes, cables and round parts, stop tuning keys by eye: record each item's shape as you put it into `A.recorder(P, { order, spring })`, let `A.settle(R, P)` measure true depth wherever two items overlap and set the keys, and end the build with `A.auditOrExit(R, P)`. Route pipes before you draw them: real ports at both ends, legs along the structure, crossings on straights, collars gapping the pipe (`references/craft.md`, section 8). Record the true shape of what you draw: a stand-in shape that silences the audit is a bug, and the audit fails one. A rod that slides out of a gland is one rod from `G.rodOut`, recorded with `A.slide`.
8. **Frame it.** Put the figure in a card: `Fig n` at top left, the structure and its cut as the title at top right, the interaction hint at bottom left (`Hover a pin · arrows step · Q quiz`) and the live readout at bottom right. The key under the card is generated from `PARTS`: the number, then the name, each in its own span so quiz mode can hide the name. The caption cites `SOURCE` and, when set, its convention. Words stay out of the drawing apart from the pins' numbers and at most one engraved label on the label plate.
9. **Make it live.** Hovering a part or its pin, or stepping with the arrow keys, pulls that part out a little on a spring, makes it the one `lit` part and writes `n · name` in the readout; a layer moves and lights with its parent. A region on a cut face is pulled out along the face normal, toward the viewer (the hippocampus section's `MOVE = [LIFT, 0, 0]` on its +x face), never up the face, where it would rise into its neighbours and fail `solidClearances` in every flight frame. Record that as the `move` of every item of the region, its prisms, cap, bands, pin, cord runs and the cord chunks that ride it (a chunk rides the region under its `(piece.from + piece.to) / 2` from `tubePieces`), and settle with states that pull one region at a time, each a `{ label, shares }` whose `shares` is keyed by piece, since the page never moves two. Selection follows `pointermove`, not `pointerover`: a pulled-out part slides out from under a parked pointer and the browser's synthetic `pointerover` on what arrives would re-select it, so `hoverAt` ignores a move over the body of the region already holding the selection, and a cord hover clears the selection so the readout shows the path. The idle tour runs a pulse along the pathways in `PATHS` order and names each path in the readout as the pulse runs it; hovering a cord runs its pulse. Q, or the toggle in the card, sets `data-quiz` on the card: the key's names hide, hover shows only the number, click or Enter reveals the name, Q again restores it. Calm time-based motion that stops when offscreen and goes still under reduced motion; attributes on refs, no re-render per frame. See `references/motion.md`.
10. **Verify like a critic.** The reader finds faults by zooming in and by checking the key against their notes, so you do both first. Read every PNG you make.
   - **Run the anatomy check to zero.** The build asserts (`check.mjs` in the hippocampus section example) that every `PARTS` row has a drawn region (a solid, or a band on its parent's face for a `layerOf` row) and a pin, every pin maps to a row, every `borders`, `layerOf`, `from` and `to` is a `PARTS` id, and it fails naming the part when any is missing. Where there is a cut face, capture it at 4× and read it beside the source's figure: every `borders` entry is a visible shared edge, and no two parts touch that the table says don't; otherwise read each `borders` entry as a visible contact at 4×. Every key name matches its source or the user's list exactly, case and spelling. Then read the source once more against the finished key.
   - Capture desktop, phone (390 wide), hover and several moments of motion with `scripts/capture.mjs`; use `scripts/drive.mjs` for sequences, the apart pose and the `--no-webgl` fallback.
   - **Run the audit to zero.** With pipes, cables or round parts, `node build.mjs --audit` must end `audit passed`, every failing line at 0: clearance at rest, apart and in mid-flight, terminals, loose ends, folds, self-overlap, crowding (against slender solids too), stand-in shapes, support, wobble, saddles and depth order. Fix an interlock in the geometry, never with a key bias.
   - **Run the line check to zero.** The audit never sees line markup. `node scripts/lines.mjs page.html --out shots/lines.png` (and on the `-light` page, and in every resting pose with `drive.mjs`'s `["lines"]` action) must print `line ends: 0`: every seam, rule, rib, tick and pointer ends on a stroke, a dot or an outline. Read the ringed PNG.
   - **4× close-ups of every junction in both themes** (build `--light` too), assembled and apart: every flange, tee, elbow, clamp, valve, bellows, band, saddle, ball joint and crossing, every curved body (no tone staircase), every place a texture ends (on an edge or fading), flat box faces with their seams, every textured hose at a chunk joint, every sliding rod at rest and at full stroke, and every window with text or shader content in it.
   - **Shaders:** a contact sheet slowed with `["slow", 0.05]` across every transition; nothing pops, no colour switch, edges read 0. In light, also log ink per frame through each transition (it must follow the light monotonically) and crop where the ink meets strokes at 8–16× (`references/verify.md`, "Light-theme shaders").
   - Put your PNG next to the examples and ask: is it instantly a real model, rich in small parts with volume, with every line ending on something and every name true? Is it highly detailed at 1× and still rewarding at 4×, with no bare face or empty corner? Iterate until yes. Checklist: `references/verify.md`.

## Setup

The kit is in `kit/`:

| File | Use it for |
|---|---|
| `kit/mesh.mjs` | The Node side of the 3D route: `packObj` parses an OBJ, welds its vertices, computes smooth normals and quantizes positions and normals into compact typed arrays, thinning any part over `maxFaces` by vertex clustering (`clusterDecimate`); `toJson` and `unpackMesh` round-trip the packed form; `zipEntries` and `zipRead` read an archive's central directory with `node:zlib`, no `unzip` binary. |
| `kit/mesh-view.js` | The browser side of the 3D route, inlined into the page: plain WebGL 1 with one shader pair, an orbit camera, a colour-ID picking framebuffer, the tags, the interactions and the readout. `mountAnatomy(stage, data)` mounts one stage and sets `window.anatomy = { parts, view(azimuthDeg, elevationDeg), pick(x, y), anchors(), frameMs }` for the checks (several stages push into `window.anatomy.stages`). |
| `kit/iso-kit.ts` | The whole core in one file: camera, solids, a parts library, an SVG string renderer, card and page builders, and the CSS (`ISO_CSS`). Copy it into a TypeScript project. |
| `kit/iso-kit.mjs` | The same core compiled to plain ESM, for Node scripts. Import it directly. The 3D route uses its `pageHtml` and `plateHtml` for the page and the card. |
| `kit/iso.css` | `ISO_CSS` written out. Import it once and put `class="iso"` (plus `data-theme="light"` for light) on a wrapper or `<body>`. |

Pick the path that fits the project:

- **A 3D figure.** Write a Node build script like `examples/brain/build.mjs`. It imports the truth table and `checkTruth`, reads the packed meshes, builds the card with `plateHtml` around the stage and the key, embeds the meshes as one JSON script, inlines `kit/mesh-view.js` in a module script that calls `mountAnatomy`, and writes the page with `pageHtml`. One file, no dependencies, offline.
- **Standalone HTML, a static SVG, a blog post or any non-React site.** Write a Node build script like `examples/dial-indicator/build.mjs`. It imports `kit/iso-kit.mjs`, assembles strings with `solidSvg`, `lineSvg`, `dotsSvg`, `wireSvg` and `faceTextSvg`, wraps them with `figureSvg` and `plateHtml`, and writes the page with `pageHtml({ script: kitScript() + live })`, or `kitScript() + glScript() + live` when it has a shader. For a lone `.svg` file use `figureSvg({ standalone: "dark" | "light" })`, which embeds the styles.
- **React or Next.js.** Copy `kit/` into the project (for example `components/iso/`). Keep geometry in plain `.ts` modules, build the static art in a server component with the `draw.tsx` components, and pass it as element props to one `"use client"` live component that moves things. `references/react.md` explains the split and a trap with large pages. A 3D figure's stage mounts the same way from a client component.

## Craft rules (short form)

These are the rules that separate a crafted figure from a diagram. `references/craft.md` gives the full reasoning and recipes for the section route; `references/neuro.md` has the tones and frames for the 3D route.

- **Solids, not wireframes.** Every part is filled in paper colour and painted in order, so things in front hide things behind. No see-through edges and no dashed hidden lines.
- **Let the kit shade.** `extrude` returns four side tones by facing plus a lighter top. Keep it for anything with height, and use `flat` only for floors and inlays thinner than 1 unit.
- **Tone hierarchy.** Base and feet are `lo`, the post, clamp and label plate are `mid` and the cast is `hi`. At most one accent is `lit` (white in dark, black in light): the hovered part, or the pulse's head chunk on the idle tour; nothing at rest. Grooves are `faint`, minor ticks `lo`, major ticks `mid` and the zero mark `hi`. Use colour only when colour is the subject, such as red, green and blue channels.
- **Small precise detail, never noise.** On the stand, use bevels of 0.4–1.6, grooves inset 3–5 from an edge, screws as 0.4–0.55 dots with a ring at the corners of plates, knurls on knobs, ruler ticks every 4–5 units with a major every 5th, and a label plate with three rule lines. Each detail belongs to a part.
- **3D figures.** Every part is its atlas mesh in the tissue tone its class gets from `references/neuro.md` (each lobe its own, insula, cerebellum, brainstem, nucleus, hippocampus, tract, cortex), lit by one key light from the camera's upper left, a fill from the other side and a soft rim so silhouettes separate; never a flat colour. The model opens in anatomical position, anterior toward the viewer, superior up, from the atlas's frame entry, as a model on a desk would. A tag sits on a visible pixel of its own part: its anchor when that is the visible surface, else the nearest visible point of the part within about 100 px, else hidden, so tags never float over the wrong part. A part that is not the subject is dimmed, greyed and darkened, never made transparent. The card chrome matches the family: the same card, title, hint, readout, caption and key as the section figures, with the canvas as the body and the background transparent so the card colour shows.
- **Tissue, not machinery.** On the section route the cast is smooth: regions are solids with no bevels, screws or knurls, drawn as tone bands with hairline borders, grey matter a tone darker than white matter, layers as bands on the cut face. Only the stand, the pins and the label plate keep the machined vocabulary.
- **Pins and key.** Every named part carries one pin: a head disc on the part (on a thin stem when the part's face is a top face), and the part's number as face text on the head. The number is text the model displays, so it doesn't count against the one-label rule. Names live in the key and the readout, never on leader lines or in the drawing.
- **Lines resolve.** Every line ends on something: a face, another line or a terminal dot. Connectors are `Wire`s whose terminal dots land on objects. There are no leader lines, balloons on leader lines, dimension arrows or labels floating in the air; a pin's numbered head stands on its part and is not a balloon.
- **No pipe or cable passes through anything.** Not through another pipe, cable or solid, at rest, apart or in any frame of the explode. Keep about 1.5 radii of clearance to everything but a line's own connection and, for a cord in a groove, the face it lies on (list those parts in the route's `touch` array); run pipes beside the stand rather than across a part's face, and run a cord along the borders of the cut face, never across a part its row does not name; gap a pipe wherever a flange, band, bellows, clamp or valve wraps it. Parts meet flush, tangent or rim-on-surface, never sunk in: a flange's back face lands on its port (on a saddle pad, `G.saddleOf`, when the port is curved), a branch leaves from a ball tee, and every end, apart too, finishes on a flange, coupling, connector shell or terminal dot.
- **Every crossing is drawn in true depth order.** Where two items overlap on screen, the nearer is drawn on top, proved by `A.settle` and the audit, not tuned by eye. Lines cross on straights, well apart in depth: a bend, end or clamp that lands on another line's outline reads as a joint (a rod, tie rod, post or rail counts as a line: a pipe's last leg never runs along one on screen), a leg that points at the viewer and then turns pinches to a V (leave such a port through an elbow ball), and a route never doubles back on screen. A pair that can't be ordered interlocks, and the fix is a cut or a tangent contact, never a bias.
- **Curved surfaces shade smoothly, never in a staircase.** Mark curved knots smooth (`[s, r, 1]`) and `lathe` shades them from one monotone curve that also draws the outline; give any curved body larger than a fitting an analytic profile with `smooth: { slope }`, cut long bodies with `G.bandsOf`, and use `G.sphereOf` for balls. Tone bands taper to nothing instead of ending on a straight cut, and seams go under the outline (`inner`).
- **A texture ends on a real edge or fades.** Ribs, bolt circles, rule lines, seams and grooves never stop in the middle of a surface, flat or curved: `ribsOf` and `dotsOf` take `fade` (draw faded ribs with `fadedSvg`), a rib row ends on its seams (`ribsOf(…, { seams: true })`), a seam or weld on a box wraps its corners (`k.sideSeam`, as the slab's `inner`), a line on one face runs edge to edge, vents are closed slots, ticks hang from a ruler strip's edge, a single line that must stop ramps out with `fadeLineSvg`, and a row whose features face up (holes on top of a ring) is seen all round (`all: true`). A texture on a pipe (braid, corrugation, wrap) follows its cross-section and runs through every chunk joint: `tubePieces(…, { rings })`, never a dashed stroke down the centreline. A pipe's shine and shade run onto its end caps and through every chunk joint. Only rule lines that stand for printed text, a needle's tail and a pointer's tip may stop on a bare face, marked `free`.
- **Shaders never pop or cut off.** Every uniform rides an envelope with an attack and a decay, phase changes are crossfades driven by the physical quantity, every term reaches zero before any bound or canvas edge, and the effect lives in world space in the figure's own camera (see below).
- **Things on faces fit them.** Text on a face (a pin's number, the label plate's engraving) fits its face at its widest string and is clipped to it; content seen through an opening is clipped by the opening as well as by its own plane, with the recess walls drawn.
- **Volume.** Give things real thickness, make them stand on other things, and put a soft halo under the base and under anything lifted.
- **Composition.** Centre the figure and let it fill about 55–65% of the card (a 3D model fills about 70% of the shorter side of its stage). Keep a consistent scale across a set of figures, and make sure it still reads at 390 px wide.

## WebGL inside the drawing

Use a shader only for what lines can't draw: fire and exhaust, water and caustics, real lamp light, glow, steam. The parts stay SVG. The core rules come from the first shader figure, whose colour change between phases looked cut rather than crossfaded:

- **Three layers.** A back SVG holds what the effect covers, the canvas holds the effect (`pointer-events: none`), and a front SVG holds what covers it. Split a part, or the effect, when it is on both sides.
- **World space, same camera.** Build every shader point from `onFloor`, `onWallX` and `onWallY`, or march the pixel's ray `rayOf(vb) + t·towardViewer()`. Size everything in world units times `uK`. Anything lying on a plane registers exactly with that plane's SVG lines.
- **Nothing pops.** Every uniform follows an envelope with an attack and a decay (the kit's `burst` for events, `settle` for levels). Phase changes are crossfades where colour shifts continuously with the physical quantity, and a fade-out only gets darker.
- **No hard cut-offs.** Every term reaches zero before any `if`, bound or canvas edge, and before any cell edge of a cell noise.
- **Seen through an opening, masked twice.** Content behind a window or a recess is masked in its own plane and by the opening in the face plane, multiplied (`references/webgl.md`, section 5).
- **Blending.** Use `tonemap()` and dither. In dark, a canvas that only adds light uses premultiplied output with `mix-blend-mode: screen`, and one that must also darken (the ripple tank's water) stays normal-blended.
- **Light theme ships, and its shaders are designed for paper.** Use normal premultiplied alpha, never screen. Write the colour the paper should show and solve for the ink. A glow is a saturated body with a pale core, a coloured rim and an alpha floor, never a darkened dark-theme colour: that gave the Raptor's "red ink smoke".
  - What the light falls on takes a tint, and the cavity that holds it gets a darker physical ground (soot, refractory, water depth).
  - Hot bodies follow one blackbody ink table shared with the fallback.
  - Ink only grows with light: fade the finished ink once, never one term at a time.
  - Ink ends at each stroke's inner edge.
  - Measure ink per frame. `references/webgl.md`, section 8, has the recipes and the faults reviewers found.
- **Restraint.** Cap the device ratio at 1.5 and render only while the effect is active and onscreen. Reduced motion gets one still frame, and the SVG must still read without WebGL.

Read `references/webgl.md` before writing any shader.

## Turning parts in a section figure (beta)

Not the atlas viewer: this is the kit's own mode for a kit-drawn part that turns in true 3D inside a section figure, an option like WebGL. Use it when a part moving in depth is the idea: a dial or turntable that turns, fingers that flex, a piece that breaks off and falls. The rest of the figure stays static kit art.

It is in beta: the engine is proved on its example and one test figure, not yet on a production figure. Turn one subject at a time and keep the clutter around it static. A small figure runs at 60 fps; a dense 380-part flexing hand measured about 20 fps at 1440 and 13–15 fps on a phone profile (390 px, 4× CPU throttle) on a heavily loaded machine.

- **Live parts are the kit's own picture.** Each frame, convex kit primitives (prisms, rounds, domes, balls with flats, lathes, tubes) are re-projected and re-shaded by the kit's rules. A turn by θ is the kit drawing at azimuth `a − θ`.
- **Order comes from separating planes.** Same-group pairs get a plane fixed in the group, pairs on different links get one fixed in their common frame, and the rest are solved each frame. The audit proves there are no cycles at any angle or in any clip frame.
- **Contacts are flush, tangent or rim-on-flat.** Joints are coaxial knuckle discs on the child group that touch the parent's link, or the audit calls the whole child group loose. No live line spans two moving groups: a cable crosses a joint through a clip on each side, or reaches the turntable through a slip ring at the hub.
- **Live tubes bend in near-horizontal planes or inside fittings, and never point along the view.** A bend needs R ≥ 2r / sin²(e − θmax), where θmax includes the pitch range of every hinge it rides: 8r is the bound when flat, and on a ±12° hinge it is 21r. Each route runs from mount surface to mount surface, with its ports in `gaps`. That holds at every angle and in every pose.
- **Materials remap the ramp.** Gold, chrome, steel, gunmetal, rubber, brass and copper each have a light and a dark set; tones still come from facing.
- **Free groups break off without a jump.** Coaxial parts are fixed pictures with turning textures.
- **Shaders follow the motion.** Anchor them to a group with `controller.anchor` or `toGroup`, occlude a front canvas with `coverOf`, and keep world effects (smoke, falling sparks, oil on the bench) out of group space.
- **Verify with `--audit`** (`turn audit passed`) and `scripts/turn-check.mjs` (`--fidelity`, `--order`, `--pops`, `--lines`, `--perf`). Iterate with `--quick --only=…`. Only a full run proves the figure, and it takes 15–20 minutes for 30 parts, so run it in the background.

Read `references/3d.md` first.
